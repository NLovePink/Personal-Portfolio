The Actionable AI Conference hosted by Pace University on September 26th, 2025 was such a valuable experience full of insights into how fast AI is evolving and how we can use it meaningfully in education, the workplace, and beyond. From the keynote, Reimagining Higher Ed in the Age of Generative AI by Charles Elliott, I learned about the many new Google AI tools and updates being implemented. He highlighted apps and features like NotebookLM, where you can upload audio files, PDFs, and more to create a personalized study companion. Guided Learning, an exciting Google Gemini feature that allows you to ask about a certain topic and receive a deep, structured explanation. He also discussed Google’s live AI tutor called Project Astra, and Google Agentspace, a tool to better analyze and interact with data. In the breakout session Data Analysis Without the Headache: AI Tools You Can Use Today by Matt Ganis, I was introduced to Julius AI, powered by ChatGPT-4. It stood out as an advanced data analytics tool that not only interprets datasets but also shows the exact Python or R code it uses, allowing you to customize it. It can even generate a full PDF analysis report—an incredible step forward in simplifying data work. During AI in the Workplace Crash Course: A Guide to Essential AI Tools with Joe Seijo and Hetal Mistry, I discovered features like Deep Research in Google Gemini, which can create a full research paper from a single prompt. They also showed Gemini’s ability to transform images into professional-quality visuals and even generate videos from still images. Joe gave memorable examples, including a pool scene turned into a creative monster animation, and later enhancing the same photo into a professional headshot. The featured session Pathways to Careers in Artificial Intelligence by Joyce Malyn Smith focused on the importance of introducing AI education earlier, starting in middle school and high school. She emphasized how young learners can explore the ways AI connects to different career paths and how educators can prepare students for a future where AI is deeply embedded in the workforce. Finally, in Enhancing Student Engagement with AI-Driven Tools and the LMS by Shannon Creighton, DBA from DSL, I learned about strategies to integrate AI into the learning management system, BrightSpace. The session addressed how AI can help educators tackle long-standing challenges in supporting student comprehension. Most importantly, AI should be used not as a shortcut for students, but as an assistant to enhance teaching and learning. Overall, this conference was an amazing experience. I walked away with a deeper understanding of how quickly AI is advancing—what was possible just six months ago already feels outdated compared to what was seen today. The pace of change is remarkable, and it reminded me how important it is to stay updated, stay curious, and keep innovating as these tools continuously evolve everyday.
